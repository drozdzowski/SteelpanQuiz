TUNING THE PAN

Building and Tuning is an extremely important and specialized skill. It is the actual creation of the steel pan. The 55-gallon drum "skirt" is cut to the desired length:

5 to 7 inches (12.7 - 17.8 cm) for a tenor pan

17 inches (43-35cm) for a cello, quadrophonic, guitar, or double seconds

17-25 inches for a tenor bass

and the full length of the drum 32-34 inches ( cm)is used for the bass pan.

Tools required for cutting the drum: an electric jigsaw or old cutlass and hammer

Tools required for sinking the drum: gloves, a marker, a compass, a 2.5 to 3kg hammer 2.5 to 3kg and earmuffs.

Tools required for marking the notes: flexible ruler and pen/marker.

Tools required for tuning: A large tuning hammer, a plastic tuning hammer, a pan mallet, a good musical ear and an electronic tuning device.

Tools required for Backing and Grooving: Metal Punch, backing sledge hammer, and a smoothing hammer with a plastic head

THE PROCESS

Decide the type of steel pan you will make.

oo Select and Clean the Drum: Start with a 55-gallon drum (typically oil or chemical). Clean the top surface, removing any residual oil, and remove any existing bends on the rim, as they will affect the sound.

o Center Punch: Mark the center of the drum head.

o Fire: Invert and burn the drum using a gas torch or open fire to prepare it for sinking and tune. Locate the dead center of the drum.

o Circle: Mark the center of the drum head with a circle.

o Sinking: Mark the circle (approx. 18–20cm) and begin the sinking process. Gradually radii 16cm, 20cm, and then trim to complete the sinking process.

o Shaping/Forming the Bowl: Using a sledgehammer or pneumatic press, refine the surface, working in a spiral from the outer circles towards the center to create a bowl shape (concave). This increases the surface tension.

o Smoothing: Use a medium-weight hammer to smooth out the surface and create a consistent, rounded bowl shape.

o Marking Notes: Outline the layout of the notes on the concave surface. Use chalk to shape the notes and isolate them from one another.

o Grooving: Define the notes by using a nail punch and hammering a groove pattern around them.

Shaping/Knocking Up: Turn the drum over and hammer the notes cut from the underside to create a convex surface for each note.

CONCERT PITCH

All instruments are tuned to concert pitch A=440Hz, which is as the standard reference for tuning instruments. Adopted officially in 1939 and confirmed by ISO 16:1975, it ensures consistency across orchestras and electronic instruments. It is the modern international standard tuning frequency for the musical note A above middle C (A4).

Steel Pan Mallets (Pan Sticks)

Steel pan mallets are made from 12mm diameter wooden dowells that you will cut to a specific length. Place latex rubber tubing at one end of each mallet. For the bass steel pans, sponge balls should be used. This is the end that strikes the notes when you play your steel pan.
